## Fuller context for the disclosure revision

The user identified the brief's restriction on wholly AI-created work and the earlier statement that independent student review and adaptation remained outstanding. The user asked the assistant to “check this recommandation and make the suggested changes”.

The assistant asked:
> What have you personally completed so far: sources read, narrative rewritten, design decisions made or code changed? Please give specific examples, or say “none yet”, so the disclosure reflects what actually happened.

The student replied:
> sources read, narrative rewritten, design decisions

The assistant then asked for named sources, a rewritten passage and one or two specific design choices. The next student reply requested the best sources, a rewritten passage and an explanation of the assistant's design decisions. The contribution record retains this distinction rather than treating a new AI draft as student writing.

## Assistant design decisions and critical revisions

The assistant chose off-white, navy and yellow, a single-page research structure, responsive typography, a code-based infographic and a visible simulation label. The assistant separated established IKEA capability from the proposed extension, deferred blockchain for the internal pilot and kept current company maturity scores unassessed. These are assistant decisions unless the student later records a specific personal review or modification.

The assistant generated the calculation model and the browser controls. Donor reserves, arrival timing and vehicle capacity constrain transfers. The model uses fictional stock and cost values and deterministic arithmetic. It does not run a trained predictive model or connect to IKEA systems.

The sample narrative rewrite added during this disclosure revision is also AI-authored. Its purpose is to demonstrate a clearer argument and preserved source attribution. Reading it or copying it does not establish that the student independently rewrote the narrative.

## AI-assisted development checks

The assistant checked public company sources for availability issues, demand sensing and dated inventory-drone deployment. Academic and official sources informed forecasting, analytics, maturity, blockchain and governance. Company disclosures remain self-reported evidence. Public abstracts support only the claims they actually contain.

Automated model checks covered no shortage, late transfers, donor protection, capacity limits, demand lost before arrival, negative economic value and invalid inputs. Browser checks exercised scenario selection, simulated approval/reset, empty-input handling, anchor links and desktop/mobile overflow. The assistant inspected PDF pages and rendered presentation slides. These are development checks, not evidence of student coding or independent review.
