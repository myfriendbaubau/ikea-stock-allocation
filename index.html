01

The business problem

Availability is a decision problem.

How could a retail planner reduce local stock shortages without creating excess inventory or unnecessary transport?

Product availability depends on where stock sits and when it can reach a customer. This project proposes decision support for a small group of IKEA retail locations operated by Ingka Group. The system would flag a likely shortage, identify a possible donor and explain the cost of moving stock. A planner would approve a transfer only after checking local commitments and delivery timing.

IKEA already uses AI demand sensing to support product planning (Ingka Group, 2023). The proposed contribution is to connect a demand warning with a feasible response. A forecast alone cannot show whether the donor can spare the product or whether a transfer will arrive soon enough. The pilot should test those constraints alongside service and total handling cost.

IKEA’s customer guidance acknowledges availability challenges and explains that checkout can fail when an item is unavailable at the distribution centre (IKEA, n.d.). This establishes a customer-facing problem. It does not measure stockout frequency, lost sales or the suitability of a specific UK store for a pilot. Those require internal evidence before implementation.

An existing digital foundation

Ingka Group describes AI demand sensing developed with Inter IKEA Systems and Blue Yonder. It reports an overall 5% improvement in forecast accuracy in Portugal, without defining the underlying accuracy metric in the release (Ingka Group, 2023). That result supports further investigation, but is not a target or a forecast of this proposal’s impact.

In August 2024, Ingka Group reported more than 250 inventory drones at 73 locations in nine countries. The company described automated checks and collaboration with workers (Ingka Group, 2024). These are dated company-reported capabilities, not evidence of universal deployment or independently verified savings.

The proposed contribution
Connect forecast exceptions to feasible stock-transfer options, with donor protection, cost checks and a recorded planner decision.

Research approach

Public company disclosures establish context. Peer-reviewed forecasting and information-systems research inform the design, while acatech and NIST guide maturity and governance. No employee interviews, live inventory access or company validation have taken place. The prototype tests transparent decision logic rather than a claim of commercial performance. The chosen module technologies are AI forecasting and data analytics, with blockchain critically evaluated as an alternative.

02

Technology analysis

Predict demand. Constrain the response.

The proposed system has two distinct jobs: estimate demand, then identify a response that is operationally possible. A good forecast cannot create stock or transport capacity.

Forecasting with useful uncertainty

A production pilot could compare a seasonal baseline with gradient-boosted trees trained on product-level sales, promotions and calendar variables. Forecasts should include uncertainty ranges. Sales during a stockout understate demand, so missing availability information can teach a model the wrong pattern. Cold-start products need explicit handling rather than confidence borrowed from unrelated lines.

Fildes et al. (2022) review the complexity of retail forecasting and comparative methods [source]. Muth et al. (2025) examine machine learning under macroeconomic volatility [source]. Together they justify a comparative trial, not an assumption that a complex model will always win. Furniture’s purchase cycle also limits the transfer of findings from frequently purchased retail categories.

Connected stock information

The design would consume existing inventory events and order commitments through controlled interfaces. Stock counts, reservations and expected arrivals need common product identifiers and timestamps. Where automated counting already exists, verified events could improve the input. Buying more sensors should follow a demonstrated information gap. Stale data should block automatic recommendations and trigger reconciliation.

Allocation with hard constraints

Candidate transfers should respect donor demand, delivery deadlines and vehicle capacity. A production optimiser could minimise expected shortage and holding costs, subject to those constraints. A planner would inspect why an option was proposed and record approval or rejection. An apparent surplus at one store is unavailable if existing orders already reserve it.

Wamba et al. (2017) found that organisational capabilities mediate the relationship between analytics and firm performance [source]. Applied here, decision rights and reliable information are essential complements to software. The proposed competitive value is a dependable customer promise with less unnecessary stock movement. That outcome remains to be tested.

Alternatives worth keeping

A threshold rule is cheap, explainable and suitable when demand is stable. A seasonal forecast handles recurring patterns with lower complexity. Machine learning may capture interactions but needs monitoring and enough relevant history. Full autonomous transfer execution introduces greater integration and governance risk, so the proposed first deployment stops at planner-approved recommendations.

Blockchain assessment

A permissioned blockchain could preserve a shared record where independently governed partners dispute transfer events. De Giovanni’s (2021) model shows that blockchain-supported coordination still depends on contractual incentives [source]. Its food-sector setting limits direct transfer to this retail case.

For a pilot within one retail operator, a controlled database with signed, append-only audit events is simpler. A ledger cannot verify that a physical count is correct, and shared governance adds cost. Blockchain is therefore evaluated as a conditional future option, not included in the prototype. Revisit it only if cross-organisation trust creates a demonstrated problem.

The proposed solution

From a warning to a reviewed decision.

  1. 01

    Reconcile

    Check available stock, reservations and timestamp quality.

  2. 02

    Anticipate

    Estimate demand until normal replenishment arrives.

  3. 03

    Compare

    Protect donor stock and test timing, capacity and cost.

  4. 04

    Review

    A planner confirms feasibility and records a reason.

A local planner receives an exception for a fast-selling shelving unit. The system shows the predicted gap and a potential donor. The planner checks handling capacity and customer commitments before approving a move. After delivery, the team compares actual demand with the forecast. Overrides become review evidence, with care to avoid teaching the model that every human intervention was correct.

Inspect the calculation and assumptions

Daily demand = baseline demand × (1 + demand change / 100). Required stock = rounded-up daily demand × replenishment days. Donor reserve = 5 units/day × the same horizon. Transfer quantity is limited by the shortage, donor surplus and vehicle capacity, and is zero if arrival is no earlier than normal replenishment.

Unmet demand before transfer arrival remains lost in this simplified model. Estimated value = avoided lost units × £15 contribution minus £25 fixed transport cost and £2.40 per transferred unit. Costs and margins are fictional assumptions. No emissions estimate is fabricated. The model omits uncertainty, multiple products, damage, reservation errors and route consolidation.

A customised digital maturity model

Readiness before automation.

The model adapts acatech’s staged capability logic to stock-allocation decisions. Its five retail-specific levels are a project design, not the original acatech scale or an audited IKEA maturity score (Schuh et al., 2020).

1

Recorded

Local records and manual decisions

2

Connected

Shared stock and common definitions

3

Visible

Trusted exceptions with named owners

4

Predictive

Validated demand and constrained options

5

Adaptive

Monitored learning with controlled authority

The same levels apply across five dimensions. A high forecast score must not conceal weak data or unsafe decision rights. Rather than averaging them into a flattering headline, the lowest critical dimension acts as the deployment gate. Level 4 is the proposed pilot target. Level 5 is conditional on evidence that greater autonomy is useful and safe.

How to apply it

Assess a defined pilot cluster through document review, system samples and planner workshops. Two assessors should record evidence and resolve disagreements. Existing demand sensing suggests relevant capability, but public releases cannot establish a complete baseline. Each dimension therefore starts as not assessed. Review scores after a pilot using the same evidence rules, with a visible explanation for every change.

Innovation lies in connecting operational prediction with accountable action and readiness gates. This is a proposed improvement in process integration rather than a claim that AI forecasting is new to IKEA. The model’s limits include judgement-based scoring and its manufacturing-derived foundation. Pilot feedback should test whether the retail dimensions capture meaningful constraints.

05

Evaluation and implementation

A pilot must earn the right to scale.

Measure the business outcome

Use a proposed six-month pilot on a small product cluster. Month one establishes stock quality and historical baselines. Months two and three run forecasts and recommendations in shadow mode, with no live execution. Months four to six allow planner-approved transfers if readiness checks pass. These are proposed stages, not IKEA’s published roadmap.

Compare a seasonal forecast, a simple threshold rule and the proposed approach using rolling time-based validation. Keep future promotions and stock events out of training inputs that would not have been known at the forecast date. Report absolute forecast error and bias, but judge allocation through fulfilment, missed demand and total handling cost. Forecast accuracy alone can improve while transfers become uneconomic.

Use a comparable product or location group where feasible, and account for promotions and seasonality. Log transfer distance and vehicle utilisation before estimating environmental effects. Finance should include software, integration, training and ongoing monitoring. Management should set success thresholds after the baseline, without inventing savings to justify the design.

Responsible and lawful operation

The National Institute of Standards and Technology’s (NIST) AI Risk Management Framework organises risk work around governance, context, measurement and management (NIST, 2023). Here, named owners should manage model errors, stale data and rollback. A stock-count mismatch should trigger investigation rather than a confident recommendation. Role-based permissions should separate recommendation, approval and execution.

The proposed inputs are aggregate product and location records. Customer identities are unnecessary for this use case. If personal information later enters the system, the organisation must review the lawful basis and applicable safeguards. The ICO’s data-minimisation guidance supports limiting personal data to what a purpose needs (Information Commissioner’s Office, n.d.). This design is not a legal compliance certification.

Planners should help design alerts and receive protected training time. Individual worker surveillance is outside scope. Transfers should protect donor customers and avoid shifting shortage risk to smaller stores without review. A service gain can increase transport emissions, so consolidation and a no-transfer option belong in the decision. Review override patterns with staff to detect alert fatigue and misplaced trust.

Scale only if

Service improves at acceptable total cost, critical maturity gates pass, and worker and environmental impacts remain acceptable. Otherwise, revise the data or workflow before adding automation.

06

Conclusion

Better allocation needs trusted judgement.

The proposal connects demand sensing with a practical question: which response is feasible, worth its cost and safe for the people making it?

IKEA’s existing forecasting and inventory technologies provide a credible foundation. The proposed extension combines constrained recommendations with planner approval and a customised readiness model. The demonstration exposes the trade-offs through simulated scenarios, while the research design specifies how a real pilot could test value. Reliable data and organisational capability remain the conditions for useful digital transformation. Company validation and measured outcomes are still needed.
